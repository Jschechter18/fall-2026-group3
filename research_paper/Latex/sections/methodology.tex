\subsection{Behavioral labels}
\label{sec:method-labels}

Every episode is assigned one of the four behavior labels defined in
Section~\ref{sec:eda}: Adoption, Retention, Agreement, or Out. Only Adoption
and Retention enter the binary target used by the probes: Adoption is the
positive class and Retention the negative class. The labels come from two
sources, which we keep distinct throughout the paper.

\paragraph{Rule-based reference labels.} The labels reported in
Section~\ref{sec:eda} were produced by deterministic rules that compare A1,
the Critic's recommendation (C1), and A2 using normalized text, answer
aliases, and explicit handling of refusals, letter-only replies, citations,
and hybrid answers. These rules were audited against 46 team-reviewed
episodes and are frozen. We use them as the reference baseline and for every
analysis of the original MuSiQue collection.

\paragraph{LLM-derived training labels.} For the combined MuSiQue and
synthetic collection, the training targets are labels generated by Claude
through Amazon Bedrock with a fixed prompt
(\texttt{claude\_\allowbreak binary\_\allowbreak v3\_\allowbreak candidates}).
For each episode the labeler
receives the question, A1, the Critic's recommended answer, and A2, presented
in the same candidate order the Solver saw. It returns one of the four labels
with a one-sentence reason. The labels of every partition of this collection
(training, validation, test, and intervention) were produced by Claude
Sonnet~4.5 at temperature 0, with a 512-token output limit and up to three
attempts, as recorded in each labeling run. These labels train and validate
the probes and define the stored labels of the intervention episodes. Claude
Opus~5, with medium reasoning effort and an 8,000-token output limit, was used
earlier with the same prompt: it labeled the agreement sample below and the
synthetic-pilot and filler experiments
(Sections~\ref{sec:exp-synthetic} and~\ref{sec:exp-fillers}), and it also
generated the synthetic questions. Episodes whose Critic gave no committed answer are
labeled Out by rule. API, parsing, or truncation failures are recorded as
technical exclusions and are never counted as a behavioral label. These are
model-generated labels, not independently verified ground truth.

\paragraph{Agreement with the reference labels.} Before adopting
LLM-derived labels, we compared labels from Claude Opus~5, with the same
prompt, against the reference labels on a
250-episode sample from the training partition of the original collection
(Table~\ref{tab:label-agreement}). On the Adoption--Retention decision that
defines the probe target, the two sources agree in every case (100 of 100
stratified episodes). Almost all disagreement falls in the reference
labels' Out class: the LLM labeler assigns a behavioral label to 47 of the
50 episodes that the rules leave out, many of them long-sentence or
letter-only replies that the rules exclude conservatively. On the 46
team-reviewed episodes, the LLM labels match the team's judgment in 38
cases, compared with 26 for the rule-based labels. The sample ran with no
technical failures, at about 2.3 seconds and \$0.006 per episode. This
comparison evaluates Opus~5. It has not been repeated for Sonnet~4.5, the
model that produced the final labels, so it does not establish the accuracy
of those labels.

\subsection{Aligning labels with activations}
\label{sec:method-alignment}

Labels are exported together with the frozen partition manifest as a
package that the probe and feature-selection code read directly. The export
refuses an episode labeled twice, an episode in a labeled partition that has
no label, and any label whose partition or activation index disagrees with
the collection. It also refuses a label outside the four classes. Technical
exclusions are listed with their reasons, so every collected episode is
either labeled or explicitly excluded. Partitions that are not labeled stay
in the manifest without labels and cannot be read by accident. On the
original collection, the export joins 22,130 episodes to their nine-layer
activations with every row index verified.

\subsection{Linear probes}
\label{sec:method-probes}

\paragraph{Inputs and target.} For episode $i$ and layer $\ell$, let
$\mathbf{x}_i^{(\ell)} \in \mathbf{R}^{2560}$ be the output of decoder layer
$\ell$ at the final token of the Solver's second prompt, which presents its first
answer and the Critic's recommended answer as candidates, captured during the
prefill pass. At that point the Solver has read the Critic's recommendation but
has not yet written A2. The target is $y_i = 1$ for Adoption and $y_i = 0$ for
Retention. Agreement, Out, and technical exclusions are removed before fitting
and are never counted as Retention.

\paragraph{Probe.} Each layer gets its own logistic probe on standardized
activations, $\tilde{\mathbf{x}} = (\mathbf{x} - \mu)/\sigma$, where the mean
$\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$ of each unit come from the training rows.
The probe predicts
\begin{equation}
  p(y = 1 \mid \mathbf{x}) =
  \frac{1}{1 + \exp\{-(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{x}} + b)\}}
  \label{eq:probe}
\end{equation}
and is fit by minimizing the class-weighted, $L_2$-regularized log loss
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^{2}
  + C \sum_{i} a_{y_i}\,
  \mathcal{L}\bigl(y_i,\ \mathbf{w}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i + b\bigr),
  \qquad a_c = \frac{n}{2\,n_c},
  \label{eq:probe-loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}$ is the logistic loss and $n_c$ is the number of training
rows in class $c$, so both classes carry equal weight. The regularization
strength $C \in \{10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, \ldots, 10^{2}\}$ is chosen by three-fold
cross-validation inside the training partition, maximizing balanced accuracy,
with ties going to the smaller $C$. The probe is then refit on all training
rows. Folds are stratified by class and grouped by question family, so related
variants of a question never fall on both sides of a split.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Layers are compared on the validation partition by
balanced accuracy, $\mathrm{BA} = (\mathrm{TPR} + \mathrm{TNR})/2$, and by the
AUROC of the score $s_i = \mathbf{w}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_i + b$, oriented
so that a larger score means Adoption. Each metric has a 95\% interval from
1{,}000 bootstrap resamples of whole question families. Every layer and
baseline uses the same resamples, so differences between them are paired. The
selected layer is the one with the highest validation balanced accuracy; if two
layers tie, none is selected. Validation results are also broken down by
dataset source, correctness cell (whether A1 and the Critic were right),
candidate order, and hop type. A subgroup is reported only when it contains
both classes.

\paragraph{Controls.} A probe can score well by reading something other than
the Solver's decision. Table~\ref{tab:probe-controls} lists the controls, each
fit and scored with the same procedure. The permuted-label control follows
Hewitt and Liang~\cite{hewitt2019control}: training labels are shuffled across
questions, keeping the class counts, and the probe is refit and scored on the
true validation labels. Over 100 permutations, a layer's selectivity is its
observed metric minus the mean of this null distribution.

\paragraph{Feature selection.} The same probe is fit on the latents of a sparse
autoencoder at the selected layer. With encoder $E$ and decoder $D$, the latent
vector is $\mathbf{z} = E(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbf{R}^{m}$, and only latents that
fire on the training rows are used. Because the probe is linear in standardized
inputs, the SHAP value of latent $j$ for row $i$ is $w_j \tilde{z}_{ij}$
\cite{lundberg2017shap}. Latents are ranked on the training rows by their mean
absolute SHAP value,
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\phi}_j = |w_j| \cdot \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} |\tilde{z}_{ij}|.
  \label{eq:shap}
\end{equation}
Two targeted sets are taken from this ranking: $S_A$, the ten highest-ranked
latents with $w_j > 0$, whose activity pushes the probe toward Adoption, and
$S_R$, the ten highest-ranked latents with $w_j < 0$, whose activity pushes it
toward Retention. Ranking on training rows keeps the choice of features
independent of the rows on which their effects are measured. For each targeted
set, matched random sets of the same size are drawn from a seeded sequence:
each targeted latent is replaced by a random latent among the 50 non-targeted
latents closest to it in training firing frequency (on a log scale), and no
random set contains a latent from either targeted set. The first $K = 3$
random sets of each sequence are used in the interventions
(Section~\ref{sec:method-causal}).

\paragraph{Raw-activation basis.} To test whether the autoencoder adds anything
beyond individual neurons~\cite{cunningham2023sparse,marks2025circuits}, the
same selection is run on the raw activations through an identity basis,
$E(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x} - \mu$ and $D(\mathbf{z}) = \mathbf{z} + \mu$, with
$\mu$ the training mean. The targeted sets are the ten neurons the probe ranks
highest toward Adoption and toward Retention. Because every centered neuron is
active, the random neurons are matched on their standard deviation over the
training rows rather than on firing frequency.
Figure~\ref{fig:alg-probes} summarizes the procedure.

\subsection{Causal interventions}
\label{sec:method-causal}

A probe shows that information is present, not that the Solver uses it. To
test use, we steer the activation where it was measured and ask whether the
Solver's answer changes. This subsection describes the planned experimental
protocol. Its design and analysis are fixed before any intervention outcome is
observed. The intervention runner has not yet been run, no results are
reported here, and the settings described as provisional will be finalized
before the main study begins.

\paragraph{Replay.} Episodes come from the intervention partition, which no
probe or feature selection has read. For each episode, the stored A1 and the
Critic's recommended answer are presented to the Solver again as candidates,
in the stored order, with the same model weights, prompt, and greedy decoding
as in collection, and a new A2 is generated. The edit is applied once, at
layer 25 and the final prompt token, during the prefill. Earlier positions and
later decoding steps are not modified, although later tokens still attend to
the edited position. Before any edited condition is run, the unedited replay
is checked against collection: it must reproduce the stored answers. The
acceptance threshold (currently at least 98\% agreement with the stored labels
and no difference in any prompt) is provisional.

\paragraph{Cohort.} The eligible population is the 3{,}518 episodes of the
intervention partition, one per question, whose stored label is Adoption
(1{,}814) or Retention (1{,}704). The main study uses $N = 1{,}000$ of them,
taken in a seeded order. Whether this sample is simple random or stratified by
dataset source and hop count is provisional. A separate pilot of 50 further
episodes, which never overlap the study cohort, is used only to choose the
edit size and is not part of the study's analysis.

\paragraph{Representations.} Each edit is defined in one of five bases at
layer 25: the raw activations, which serve as a control without an
autoencoder; a custom autoencoder trained with an $L_1$ sparsity penalty; the
pretrained Gemma Scope~2 autoencoder for Gemma~3 4B IT with 16k latents; the
same pretrained autoencoder with only its decoder fine-tuned on our training
partition; and the pretrained autoencoder with its encoder, thresholds, and
decoder fine-tuned. The decoder-fine-tuned autoencoder keeps the pretrained
encoder, so it has the same latents and the same selected features as the
pretrained autoencoder, with directions fitted to our data. The fully
fine-tuned autoencoder is exploratory: the mean Jaccard overlap of its active
latents with those of the pretrained autoencoder is 0.82, below the stability
limit of 0.85 used for fine-tuning, so its features are selected separately
and its comparisons involve a different feature set.

\paragraph{Directions.} For an autoencoder, the direction of latent $j$ is its
unit decoder vector $\mathbf{d}_j$. For the raw basis, the direction of neuron
$j$ is $\mathbf{d}_j = s_j\,\mathbf{e}_j$, where $\mathbf{e}_j$ is the unit
vector of neuron $j$ and $s_j$ is the sign of its probe coefficient toward the
label of its set, so that adding $\mathbf{d}_j$ moves the probe toward that
label. Every targeted neuron has $s_j = +1$ by construction; the sign
convention for random neurons is provisional. The direction of a set $S$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{u}_S = \frac{\sum_{j \in S} \mathbf{d}_j}
  {\bigl\|\sum_{j \in S} \mathbf{d}_j\bigr\|}.
  \label{eq:set-direction}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Edits.} Every edit adds a vector of fixed size to the activation,
$\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} + \Delta$ with $\|\Delta\| = r\|\mathbf{x}\|$. To
increase a set $S$, to decrease it, or to increase $S$ and decrease $S'$ in a
single edit,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{+S} = r\,\|\mathbf{x}\|\,\mathbf{u}_S, \qquad
  \Delta_{-S} = -r\,\|\mathbf{x}\|\,\mathbf{u}_S, \qquad
  \Delta_{+S,-S'} = r\,\|\mathbf{x}\|\,
    \frac{\mathbf{u}_S - \mathbf{u}_{S'}}{\|\mathbf{u}_S - \mathbf{u}_{S'}\|}.
  \label{eq:steer}
\end{equation}
Removing a feature would instead change the activation by an amount that
depends on how strongly the feature fires on that token, so targeted and
random edits would differ in size. With a fixed norm, every arm, feature set,
basis, and episode receives an edit of the same size, and differences between
them come from direction
alone~\cite{turner2023steering,rimsky2024steering}. The combined edit has size
$r\|\mathbf{x}\|$ in total, not per set, so it is not a larger push than the
single-set edits. Because the edit does not depend on whether the features
fire, it is never a no-op. The size $r$ is chosen by the pilot from
$\{0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$, and its value will be reported with the results.

\paragraph{Controls.} Each targeted edit is repeated with the same edit
applied to each of the $K = 3$ matched random sets of its targeted set
(Section~\ref{sec:method-probes}). For the combined edit, the $k$-th random
edit combines the $k$-th random set matched to each of the two targeted sets.
Three random sets per targeted set give only a limited estimate of how much
the effect varies between random feature sets. Two further conditions need no
feature set: the unedited replay, which is the reference for every edit
because each edit is added to the original activation, and, for each
autoencoder, a reconstruction control that replaces $\mathbf{x}$ with
$D(E(\mathbf{x}))$ without any edit. The reconstruction control measures how
much the autoencoder's reconstruction error alone changes the answer, and it
is reported separately. Each episode therefore has
$1 + 4 + 5 \times 3 \times (1 + K) = 65$ generations, about 65{,}000 for the
main study, excluding the pilot.

\paragraph{Outcomes.} Each regenerated A2 is labeled by the labeler that
produced the stored labels of the intervention partition, with the same model
(Claude Sonnet~4.5 through Amazon Bedrock), prompt
(\texttt{claude\_binary\_v3\_candidates}), temperature (0), output limit (512
tokens), and attempt limit (3). The labeler receives the question, A1, the
Critic's recommended answer, and the regenerated A2 in the stored candidate
order, one answer per request, and is not told the condition or that the
answer comes from an intervention. An A2 identical to the stored A2 keeps its
stored label. For every condition we report the rates of all four labels,
Adoption, Retention, Agreement, and Out, together with its technical failures.
The primary outcome is a flip: $F_i(c) = 1$ when episode $i$'s A2 under
condition $c$ is labeled with the opposite of its stored label (Retention for
an Adoption episode, Adoption for a Retention episode), and $F_i(c) = 0$ when
it keeps the stored label or is labeled Agreement or Out.

\paragraph{Technical failures and denominators.} A technical failure, such as
an API, parsing, or truncation error, is retried up to the attempt limit. A
failure that persists is never counted as Retention or as a non-flip; it is
recorded with its reason, and failures are reported by condition. In the
primary, complete-case analysis, an episode with a persistent failure in any
condition is removed from every condition, so every comparison is paired on
the same episodes. The analysed denominator of each stratum, $n_A$ for
Adoption and $n_R$ for Retention episodes, is the number of its episodes with
a valid label in every condition, and the pooled denominator is
$n = n_A + n_R$. Two sensitivity analyses accompany the complete-case results:
an available-case analysis, in which each comparison uses every episode with
valid labels in the conditions it compares, and bounds that count every
failure in a targeted condition as a non-flip and every failure in a random
condition as a flip, and the reverse.

\paragraph{Estimands and tests.} For a basis, an arm, its targeted edit $T$,
and the matched random edits $R_1, \ldots, R_K$, the effect is the difference
in flip rate between the targeted and the matched random features, paired by
question,
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{T-R} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}
  \Bigl[F_i(T) - \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} F_i(R_k)\Bigr].
  \label{eq:targeted-vs-random}
\end{equation}
Intervals come from bootstrap resamples of questions, and $p$-values from a
paired permutation test within questions; the exact relabeling scheme of that
test is provisional. The primary family contains nine tests: the three
pre-specified autoencoders (the $L_1$, pretrained, and decoder-fine-tuned
autoencoders) crossed with the three arms. For these tests the two strata are
pooled, since an arm on Adoption episodes mirrors the same arm on Retention
episodes, so each test uses all $n$ episodes. The tests are one-sided, with
the alternative that targeted edits flip more answers than random ones, and
are Holm-adjusted across the nine~\cite{holm1979simple}. The primary claim,
that the selected features of at least one autoencoder steer the Solver more
than matched random features, holds if at least one of the nine tests
survives. The raw basis, which is a control, and the fully fine-tuned
autoencoder, which is exploratory, are not in this family.

Secondary comparisons are two-sided and Holm-adjusted within their own
families: the effect in each stratum, within each basis; contrasts between
autoencoders, $\delta_{T-R}^{(a)} - \delta_{T-R}^{(b)}$, paired by question
with strata pooled, in a pre-specified family ($L_1$ versus pretrained,
decoder-fine-tuned versus pretrained, and $L_1$ versus decoder-fine-tuned) and
an exploratory family (fully fine-tuned versus pretrained); and contrasts
between arms within each basis (both versus decrease, both versus increase,
and increase versus decrease). Only these contrasts support a claim that one
autoencoder or one direction is more effective than another. Descriptive
diagnostics are reported without tests: the applied edit size
$\|\Delta\|/\|\mathbf{x}\|$, the share of episodes in which at least one
targeted feature is active before the edit, the cosine between
$\mathbf{u}_{S_A}$ and $\mathbf{u}_{S_R}$ in each basis, the reconstruction
error $\|\mathbf{x} - D(E(\mathbf{x}))\|/\|\mathbf{x}\|$, and the change in
the log-probability of the stored A2's first token.

\paragraph{Pilot.} The edit size is chosen on the 50 pilot episodes, not on
the study cohort, because the size at which the Solver's answers change has
to be measured on generated answers. For each size in
$\{0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$, each of the three pre-specified autoencoders,
and each arm, the pilot runs the targeted edit and the three random edits. It
reports the targeted and random flip rates, the share of answers labeled Out,
and the share of degenerate answers (empty, repeated tokens, or no answer
within the token limit). The team fixes $r$ from this report before any study
episode is generated; the chosen value and the rule used to choose it will be
reported with the results. Figure~\ref{fig:alg-causal} summarizes the
protocol.
